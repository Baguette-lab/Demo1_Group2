# Sa Iisang Bubong — the rehearsal script

The six-part Mika and Eli skit follows the supplied group PDF. A duplicated Part 2 narrator paragraph appears once, and narration is divided between two readers. Music transitions use only the seven songs in the group’s final supplied list.

Roles: Narrator 1, Narrator 2, Mika, Eli.

## Chapter 1: Paglapit — Why one home makes sense

Month 0 · Before the move

**Narrator 1:** Mika, 24, is a contract designer. Eli, 25, works rotating support shifts. After three years together, most dates still happen between long commutes.

**Mika:** By the time I get home, you’re leaving for work. I want us to have a normal dinner, not just messages saying we miss each other.

**Eli:** This room is closer to both jobs. We could share rent and groceries—and still send money home. But I want this because I want to be with you, too.

**Mika:** I want that. I’m not ready for a wedding or children yet. Can we live together without pretending those decisions are already made?

**Eli:** Yes. I do hope we’ll marry someday. Let’s talk about what “someday” means instead of assuming we mean the same thing.

**Narrator 2:** They move in unmarried. Sharing costs matters, but so do affection, independence, and learning what everyday partnership asks of them.

**Music transition:** Panalangin voices their hope of staying close. When I Met You gives that hope its emotional beginning. Their affection makes a shared home meaningful; marriage and children remain separate decisions.

**Play Panalangin — 38.28 seconds:** They hope a shared home can become a lasting life. Hear that promise as a hope they sincerely share—not a wedding or a settled decision about children.

**Play When I Met You — 24.91 seconds:** Before Mika and Eli share a key, they choose each other. This first cut gives their wish for ordinary time together its emotional beginning.

**Demographic lens:** Cohabitation · household formation

## Chapter 2: Pagsasama — The work behind “ours”

Month 3 · Everyday life

**Narrator 1:** For a while, they make the schedules work. Eli leaves breakfast before sleeping; Mika packs his dinner before her own deadlines.

**Mika:** Lately I’m cooking, tracking the bills, and reminding you what we need. I’m tired too. I don’t want to become the manager of our home.

**Eli:** My shifts changed, and I’m helping with my brother’s school fees. But I used being tired as a reason to leave everything else to you. I’m sorry.

**Mika:** I’m not asking you to stop helping your family. I’m asking us to decide what we can both carry, instead of letting it fall to whoever notices first.

**Eli:** Let’s put the chores and expenses where we can both see them. I’ll handle the groceries and laundry. We’ll protect one evening a week for us.

**Narrator 2:** The chart helps. For the next few months they try to share care more fairly. Repair is possible—but practical agreements cannot answer every question about a future.

**Music transition:** The problem is not a single unwashed plate. It is the feeling of becoming the only person who notices what the home needs. Vines leaves room for apology and practical repair.

**Play Vines — 12.95 seconds:** Mika names the work of noticing and remembering. Eli apologizes, and they try to share care more fairly. This cut gives that attempt at repair a voice.

**Demographic lens:** Household well-being · division of care

## Chapter 3: Pagkakaiba — They meant different things by “later”

Month 6 · The plans beneath the plans

**Narrator 1:** Six months in, the dishes are no longer the main issue. Mika’s contract will end later in the year. She starts applying for stable work in Iloilo, near her family.

**Mika:** If I get the job, I want to take it. I also want a few years to build my career. I’m still unsure whether I want children.

**Eli:** I thought we were saving for a wedding, then a family here. My work is here, and my brother still needs me nearby. I didn’t realize your picture was that different.

**Mika:** When I said “later,” I meant I hadn’t decided. You heard a promise with a date attached. I should have made that clearer.

**Eli:** I should have asked. Moving in felt like we were already on the same path, so I stopped checking where each of us actually wanted to go.

**Narrator 2:** Neither plan is wrong. But avoiding the difference makes the home lonelier. They still share an address while important conversations become messages left unanswered.

**Music transition:** True Love Waits gives devotion and sacrifice a voice. Mika and Eli must ask which promises they can freely make, and which would require one person to surrender the future they want.

**Play True Love Waits — 23.12 seconds:** Their ideas of marriage, children, and location differ. These promises let us ask when devotion becomes a sacrifice of someone’s own future.

**Demographic lens:** Life course · expectations · linked lives

## Chapter 4: Pagpapasya — An ending they do not rush

Month 9 · After trying to repair

**Narrator 1:** They keep the weekly conversations for three more months. Some days feel close again. Then Mika receives the Iloilo offer, and they have to stop treating their future as a distant question.

**Eli:** Could we try living apart for work? I don’t want to call this over just because a job is in another city.

**Mika:** We could manage the distance. But it wouldn’t settle marriage or children. I can’t ask you to wait for a promise I may never be able to make.

**Eli:** And I can’t stay while quietly expecting you to change your mind. That would turn every good thing between us into something we’re bargaining with.

**Mika:** Then let’s not renew the lease together. We can love what we built and still accept that we can’t keep building the same life.

**Narrator 2:** They choose to end the relationship when the lease ends. This is their response to persistent differences after trying to repair—not a rule about what every couple should do.

**Music transition:** Unti-Unti gives emotional distance a gradual pace. After trying to repair the relationship, they acknowledge that sharing an address cannot make their plans agree.

**Play Unti-Unti — 22.67 seconds:** Even after practical repairs, their plans remain different. The fading words and promises give emotional distance a gradual pace before they decide to part.

**Demographic lens:** Union dissolution · agency

## Chapter 5: Pag-aayos — Leaving is also a practical task

Month 12 · The lease ends

**Narrator 1:** At the end of the lease, there are final bills, a deposit, and belongings neither person can pretend are only sentimental. They make a list before they start packing.

**Mika:** The deposit came from both of us. Let’s settle the utilities first, then divide what’s left. I’ll take the desk; you keep the table you brought from home.

**Eli:** My cousin has room while I save for another place near work. You’ll stay with your family until the new job starts. We don’t have to solve the whole next year tonight.

**Mika:** I’ll miss you. But let’s give each other space instead of acting like nothing changed every time one of us feels lonely.

**Eli:** You were good to me in ways I didn’t always notice. I’m sorry for that. I can be grateful without asking you to stay.

**Narrator 2:** They return the key. Two people have not disappeared from the population; a shared household has changed, and their relatives now become part of each person’s support network.

**Music transition:** The Apartment We Won’t Share grieves an imagined future. In the skit, that loss also has practical consequences: final bills, divided belongings, and a returned key.

**Play The Apartment We Won’t Share — 19.05 seconds:** The future they imagined now belongs to different lives. The selected ending connects grief with accepting an ending they did not want.

**Demographic lens:** Household reorganization · support networks

## Chapter 6: Pagpapatuloy — Two addresses, unfinished feelings

Six months later · Separate lives

**Narrator 1:** Six months later, Mika is working in Iloilo. Eli is still in Metro Manila. They have new routines, but neither person feels completely fine on a neat schedule.

**Eli:** Some days I say I’m doing great. Then I start a message to you and remember that missing someone isn’t the same as having a different answer to what ended us.

**Mika:** The plant from our window is beside my new desk. I’m glad I took this job. I still miss the evenings when the apartment felt like ours.

**Narrator 2:** They moved in for love, more time, and a manageable shared life. They left after repair revealed different plans they could not honestly promise to one another.

**Narrator 2:** They were not married, so this story is about cohabitation and the end of a union. It should not be counted as a legal divorce.

**Narrator 2:** Their story opens a wider question for our closing discussion: when a marriage can no longer be sustained, what choices and support should help people leave with dignity?

**Music transition:** Sa Susunod na Habang Buhay returns to promises and the home they worked to build. Their new addresses arrive before every feeling catches up.

**Play Sa Susunod na Habang Buhay — 40.66 seconds:** They remember the promises and effort invested in their shared home. This passage holds that loss beside the separate lives they are learning to build.

**Demographic lens:** Post-separation adjustment · life course
